What all of these have in common

They are all personal-input apps: you give a birth date (and often a time and place), and the app returns material about you. The differences that matter are not the interface, they are what goes in and what comes back out.

  • Input: birth data alone, or birth data plus something you did — a draw, a question, a note.
  • Output: a forecast, a description of your patterns, or a reading of a specific draw.
  • Memory: whether the app remembers what it told you last week and uses it.
  • Tone: prediction ("here is what will happen") versus reflection ("here is the pattern to look at").

Tarosyn is deliberately in the reflection column, and its memory is the feature it is built around. If you want a forecast, the horoscope apps do that job better than we do — and we say so on both comparison pages.

How to choose in one line

If you want to know what today looks like for your sign, open a horoscope app. If you want to understand a card you just drew — and have the next reading remember it — that is what Tarosyn is for.
