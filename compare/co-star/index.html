Tarosyn vs Co-Star

Short answer: if you want today's horoscope for your sign, use Co-Star. If you want to read a card you just drew — in the context of your chart and everything you have drawn before — use Tarosyn.

They share an input (your birth data) and almost nothing else.

What Co-Star is

Co-Star describes itself as hyper-personalized, real-time horoscopes. Its centre of gravity is the daily read: your chart plus current planetary movement, delivered as a short, opinionated horoscope. That is a real craft and a different product from a tarot app.

What Tarosyn is

Tarosyn is a tarot practice with memory. You draw a card or a spread; the reading is written against your natal chart, the cards that keep returning for you, and notes you have saved. Nothing about it claims to know your future — the output is a reading of the draw, not a forecast for the day.

Who should pick which

  • Pick Co-Star if the value you want is a daily horoscope you can read in ten seconds, written about your chart rather than your sun sign alone.
  • Pick Tarosyn if you already pull cards, keep a tarot journal, or want the draw itself interpreted — and want the app to remember what you drew last time.
  • You can reasonably use both, and plenty of people do: one app for the sky, one for the cards.

Questions people ask

Is Tarosyn a replacement for Co-Star?

No. A horoscope and a tarot reading are different instruments. Tarosyn does not generate daily horoscopes for your sign; that is what the free horoscope page is for, and it is a separate surface from the app's reading practice.

Which one remembers my readings?

Tarosyn does, by design: saved readings and reflections are part of your vault and can inform the next reading.

Do I need to know tarot to use Tarosyn?

No. Cards are explained in plain language, and the reading is written for you rather than requiring you to interpret symbols first.
