How to read tarot cards in combination

Start with position. Each slot in a spread assigns the card a role. Past, present, future is the classic three, but situation / obstacle / advice or mind / body / spirit work the same way — the card answers its position first, the question second.

Look for the through-line. Repeated suits concentrate the story: two or more Cups means this is an emotional matter at its core, whatever the question was; Wands make it about energy and ambition; Swords about truth and conflict; Pentacles about the material world. Multiple Major Arcana cards mark a chapter-level event — bigger than habit, slower than a mood. Climbing numbers (a 3, then a 5, then an 8) usually signal building momentum; falling numbers suggest something completing or releasing.

Read the tension, not just the cards. Adjacent cards interact. Fire (Wands) and Water (Cups) sitting next to each other describe drive and feeling working against each other; Fire beside Air (Swords) feeds each other. The middle card is the hinge — read how you get from the first card to the third, and the middle card tells you what it costs.

Treat the future position as trajectory, not prophecy. A spread shows where current energy leads if nothing changes. That’s what makes it useful: you’re the variable it can’t account for.
