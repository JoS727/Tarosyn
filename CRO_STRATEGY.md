# Tarosyn landing-page CRO strategy

## Conversion brief

- **Primary audience:** spiritually curious adults who want tarot or astrology to feel personal and useful, but may distrust generic AI readings, exaggerated mystical claims, or subscription traps.
- **Primary job:** help a first-time visitor understand why Tarosyn is meaningfully different and start a free reading at `tarosyn.app`.
- **Primary conversion:** click a `Start your free reading` or equivalent free-start CTA.
- **Secondary conversions:** inspect how the product works, view pricing, sign in, or read the public project record.
- **Offer:** a first Tarosyn experience in about 90 seconds, free to start, with no payment card required.
- **Positioning:** Tarosyn is a reflective practice with memory. Birth-chart context, past cards, and private reflections make future readings more relevant over time.
- **Core differentiator:** the product compounds. A generic tarot result is disposable; a Tarosyn practice becomes more useful as it remembers the seeker’s context.

## Copy architecture

### Primary promise

> Tarot that gets more personal every time you return.

This is stronger than a feature-led headline because it communicates the outcome and the compounding advantage in one sentence.

### Mechanism statement

> Tarosyn weaves your birth chart, past cards, and private reflections into guidance that remembers where you’ve been—and meets you where you are now.

The mechanism makes the promise believable. “Personalized” alone is an empty category claim; the chart, history, and reflection layers explain why the result changes.

### Offer statement

> Free to start. No card required. About 90 seconds.

These three points should remain adjacent to the CTA anywhere the visitor is making a first-use decision.

### Voice principles

1. Mystical, but grounded.
2. Personal, but never invasive.
3. Reflective, never deterministic.
4. Specific, never hype-led.
5. Inviting, never urgency-manipulative.

## CTA strategy

### Hierarchy

- **Primary:** `Start your free reading`
- **Compact primary:** `Start free`
- **Feature-specific:** `Forge your first Hero Card`
- **Secondary:** `See how it works`
- **Returning user:** `Sign in`

The primary CTA is a low-friction behavior, not a subscription commitment. Pricing CTAs deliberately route visitors back through the free experience because the product should earn the upgrade.

## Mystic pricing decision

- **U.S. list price:** `$7.99/month`
- **Annual anchor:** `$59.99/year`, equivalent to about `$5/month`
- **Annual saving:** `37%` versus twelve Mystic monthly payments (`$95.88`)

The $7.99 price is intentionally one step below the dominant $9.99 North American monthly subscription anchor. It keeps Mystic accessible enough to convert spiritually curious users who are not ready for a premium commitment, while avoiding the volume-dependent economics of the roughly $6.67 low-price cohort.

The market context supports this position: RevenueCat reports a $9.99 North American monthly median and a $6.67 median for low-priced apps; Labyrinthos lists a $9.99 monthly tarot subscription; and CHANI lists premium at $11.99 monthly. Mystic therefore enters below the clearest direct substitutes without signaling a disposable or ad-supported product.

At standard payment costs, $7.99 leaves approximately `$6.79` after a 15% app-store fee or `$7.46` after Stripe's 2.9% + 30¢ U.S. online-card fee, before taxes, AI inference, support, and other variable costs. Monitor first-reading-to-paid conversion, first monthly renewal, refund rate, and contribution margin before testing a lower price.

Sources: [RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026-utilities), [Labyrinthos App Store listing](https://labyrinthos.app/), [CHANI pricing](https://chaninicholas.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/1500001732281-App-Pricing), [Apple Small Business Program](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/), [Google Play service fees](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/112622), and [Stripe pricing](https://stripe.com/pricing).

### Placements

1. Navigation: persistent compact CTA.
2. Hero: full primary CTA next to a “See how it works” alternative.
3. After three-step explanation: converts visitors whose concern was complexity.
4. Hero Card section: feature-specific CTA for identity/creative motivation.
5. Each pricing plan: free start remains the first behavior.
6. Final section: strongest emotional close.
7. Mobile sticky bar: continuous small-screen access.

### Measurement contract

Every conversion link has a `data-cta` position. `script.js` emits both:

```js
window.dispatchEvent(new CustomEvent('tarosyn:cta', {
  detail: { position, destination }
}));
```

and, when a `dataLayer` exists:

```js
dataLayer.push({ event: 'cta_click', cta_position: position });
```

Recommended funnel events:

1. `landing_view`
2. `cta_click` with `cta_position`
3. `onboarding_started`
4. `question_submitted`
5. `first_reading_completed`
6. `account_created`
7. `trial_started`
8. `subscription_started`

The business KPI is **first reading completed per unique landing visitor**, not raw CTA click-through rate. CTA CTR is diagnostic; completion is the value moment.

## Social proof policy

The repository contains no verified customer quotes, reviewer identities, star counts, or outcome data. The shipped page therefore does not invent testimonials. Its current proof stack is:

- a visible photo-to-Hero-Card transformation;
- concrete feature artifacts and product counts;
- free trial behavior before payment;
- a public living project record;
- explicit “reflective, not absolute” product principles;
- clear pricing and cancellation language.

When verified feedback exists, replace or extend the “Early access. Honest proof.” section with three short testimonials. Each should include the speaker’s approved first name, photo or credible identity marker, use case, and the specific before/after change. Avoid unqualified claims such as “changed my life.” Collect permission and preserve the original wording.

Recommended testimonial prompts:

1. “What felt different from other online readings?”
2. “What made you return for a second or third session?”
3. “What did the saved history or birth-chart context help you notice?”

## Interaction and responsive guidance

- The page is mobile-first from 320px upward, with a full-screen accessible mobile menu and a thumb-reachable sticky CTA below 620px.
- Content stacks in decision order on smaller screens; the free plan remains first, followed by annual value and monthly flexibility.
- Reveal animations run once, use short travel distances, and respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- CTA hover/tap feedback is tactile but does not create motion that competes with the copy.
- FAQ items use native `details` elements for keyboard behavior and graceful no-JavaScript fallback.
- Images use local WebP sources with PNG/JPEG fallbacks and lazy loading below the fold.
- Focus styles, a skip link, landmark navigation, descriptive alt text, and 44px-plus controls support keyboard and touch access.
- If JavaScript fails, all core content, links, pricing, and FAQs remain usable.

## Credibility and claim guardrails

- Keep “about 90 seconds,” “eight guides,” pricing, and trial terms synchronized with the application.
- Do not publish user counts, star ratings, outcome percentages, or testimonials until they can be verified.
- Do not imply medical, legal, financial, therapeutic, psychic, or supernatural certainty.
- Keep the entertainment and self-reflection disclaimer visible in the FAQ and footer.
- If birth-data handling differs from the privacy policy, update the FAQ before deployment.
- Verify that every free and paid entitlement matches the live product before merging.

## Recommended experiment backlog

Run one meaningful variable at a time and evaluate downstream reading completion, not only clicks.

1. **Hero message:** “more personal every time you return” vs. “the tarot practice that remembers you.”
2. **Hero visual:** cosmic card deck vs. genuine Hero Card before/after.
3. **Primary CTA:** “Start your free reading” vs. “Ask your first question.”
4. **Proof order:** three-step explainer before comparison vs. current comparison-first order.
5. **Offer framing:** “no card required” vs. “no account required,” only if the latter is operationally true.
6. **Pricing visibility:** current full pricing vs. a collapsed “See upgrade options” treatment.
7. **Verified social proof:** product proof only vs. product proof plus three approved seeker stories.

## Pre-merge checklist

- Confirm all pricing and trial claims in the live app.
- Confirm the free flow does not request a payment card.
- Confirm the first reading can reasonably be completed in about 90 seconds.
- Confirm there are eight available guides.
- Verify `tarosyn.app` and login destinations.
- Verify privacy and terms URLs.
- Verify the mobile menu, CTA tracking events, FAQ accordion, focus order, and reduced-motion mode.
- Test 320px, 390px, 768px, 1024px, and 1440px widths.
- Check for horizontal overflow and image layout shift.
- Replace the Open Graph image if a stronger campaign-specific social card becomes available.
